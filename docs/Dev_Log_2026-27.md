## 2026-08-21 — Super 6 correct-score picks: coded baseline

**Branch:** `claude/super-6-correct-score-o9nxd2` · **PR:** [#7](https://github.com/ferret-stack/odds-calculator/pull/7)

**Goal:** Build the first end-to-end Super 6 pass — fixtures in, a correct-score pick per fixture out, written to a structured file. Picks up exactly where the Poisson validation pass stopped: that entry flagged "Super 6 scoreline-picking / tie-break logic" as explicitly out of scope, and this is that logic. Super 6 is a fixed-cost jackpot punt, not a priced bet, so it stays entirely clear of the +EV pipeline: no staking, no bankroll, no ledger, and `calculate_poisson()` is imported and called, never modified.

### The pick rule, and why the argmax alone was not enough

The pick is the argmax of the score matrix, with one adjustment. Poisson grids routinely put several scorelines within a few tenths of a percentage point of each other, which makes a bare argmax close to arbitrary between them. Brighton v Man Utd is the live example from this run: 1-1 at 11.93% and 1-0 at 11.78% — a 0.15-point gap that carries no real information about which is likelier.

So any scoreline within **0.005 absolute (0.5 percentage points)** of the argmax counts as tied with it, and among those the lower-scoring outcome wins. Half a point is wide enough to catch the genuine near-ties and narrow enough that a scoreline the model actually prefers is not displaced by a materially less likely one. Ties break by fewest total goals, then highest probability (separates 1-0 from 0-1), then lowest home and away goals so the same matrix always yields the same pick.

The rule fired once in ten: Brighton v Man Utd, 1-1 → 1-0. It also correctly held 2-0 over a near-tied 3-0 on Man City v Aston Villa, and 1-0 over 1-1 on Nott'm Forest v Bournemouth. The tolerance, the ordering, the argmax, and the passed-over candidates are all recorded in the output file — the picks are not reproducible from the matrix alone, so a reader needs the rule to see why a non-argmax scoreline was chosen.

### What was built

`tools/super6_picks.py` — standalone script, matching the `tools/` convention rather than living in `pipeline/`, which is reserved for the recurring end-to-end run. Writes `data/super6_output.json` following the `build_report()` shape in `run_pipeline.py`: `generated_at` as a space-separated local timestamp, snake_case keys, nested blocks, a `totals` summary.

It runs **every** fixture in `upcoming_fixtures.json`, not six. Choosing which six make the official slate is a manual call; `--limit N` exists for test runs and has no default. A fixture whose matrix comes back empty (`calculate_poisson()` returns `{'matrix': []}` for a team missing from team stats) is skipped and recorded rather than taking the slate down with it — none hit that path this run.

### The narrative layer is a placeholder

One `requests.post` to a local Ollama instance, no retries, no configurable template. Deliberately thin: the real narrative architecture — model choice, failure handling, command structure — is undesigned, and anything built here gets thrown away. A failed call yields `narrative: null` plus the recorded reason, never a failed run.

That path is the one that actually executed. Ollama was not reachable from the build environment, so all ten picks shipped with a null narrative and a logged `ConnectionError`. The failure path is therefore verified end-to-end, which is worth something; the success path is not, and needs a run on a machine with Ollama up. Model tag defaults to `llama3.2:3b` and is overridable via `--model`.

### Verified

All ten matrices sum to ~1.0 (worst deviation 0.000004, consistent with the validation pass). Picks span a sensible range — 0-0 through 2-1 — and the high single-scoreline probabilities (0-1 at 20.1% on West Ham v Leeds) track genuinely low expected goals rather than a bug: that fixture prices at 0.59-1.20 xG. `git diff` confirms nothing under `pipeline/` and no line of `odds_calculator.py` was touched.

### Status

`python3 tools/super6_picks.py` produces ten real picks from real fixture data. Outstanding: a run with Ollama live to exercise the narrative success path, and the real LLM architecture pass.

## 2026-08-21 — Poisson score-matrix validation

**Branch:** `claude/poisson-matrix-validation-7vbyer` · **PR:** not yet opened

**Goal:** `calculate_poisson()` (`odds_calculator.py`) generates the score matrix Super 6 (correct-score jackpot) will depend on directly. It had never been tested for its own mathematical correctness — this was a standalone validation pass, not part of the Super 6 build.

### What was found

Wrote `tools/validate_poisson.py` — a reusable regression check (`validate_score_matrix()` as a pure function, plus a script mode) confirming for a given matrix that all probabilities sum to ~1.0 (±0.001), none is negative, and none exceeds 1.0. Run against the 10 real fixtures in `data/upcoming_fixtures.json`: negative/>1.0 never fired, but **9 of 10 fixtures failed the sum check**, worst case Man City v Aston Villa at 0.9251 — 7.5% of probability mass missing.

Root cause: the matrix was a 6×6 grid (0-5 goals per side) that truncated the joint Poisson distribution without renormalizing — any scoreline with 6+ goals on either side was simply absent. Not a pmf bug. The deficit scaled with expected goals, so it was worst exactly where Super 6 would care most. Also confirmed `calculate_poisson()` has no callers anywhere in the repo — it does not feed O/U, BTTS, or any existing market pricing, so the fix carried zero regression risk to live markets.

### What was fixed

Widened the grid twice, each step re-validated against the same real fixtures before moving on:

- **0-5 → 0-10.** Cut the worst deviation from 0.0749 to 0.0006. Confirmed by direct comparison that the original cells were extended, not rescaled — every 0-5 cell stayed bit-identical.
- **0-10 → 0-15, with rounding raised from 4dp to 6dp.** A λ sweep surfaced that the two error sources move in opposite directions as the grid grows: truncation shrinks, but rounding noise grows with cell count. At 0-15 with 4dp, rounding noise alone would have breached tolerance in the high-xG range (worst 0.0014 across a 40-point λ sweep) — widening the range without also tightening the rounding would have made the fix incomplete. At 6dp: 0 of 40 λ combinations breach tolerance, worst deviation across real fixtures **0.000004**.

Verified at every step: negative/>1.0 checks still pass, `calculate_poisson()` still has no callers, `elo_calculator.py` and the band-frequency logic driving O/U/BTTS untouched, and the pre-existing test suite (21 failed / 186 passed — unrelated `test_cookie_dismissal.py` browser failures) shows zero delta from the change.

### Flagged, not touched

Super 6 scoreline-picking / tie-break logic and wiring `calculate_poisson()` into any market were explicitly out of scope for this pass.

### Status

`tools/validate_poisson.py` passes on all 10 real fixtures. Reusable as a regression check going forward.
## 2026-08-20 — Idempotency guard, and a readiness check before the season loads

**Branch:** `claude/pensive-cerf-xd0whp` · **PR:** not yet opened

**Goal:** Close the idempotency hole flagged at the end of the settlement session and then take stock, because the two are the same question. `run_pipeline` wrote bets with no check of any kind — `for d in decisions: if d.bet: ledger.place(d)` — so a second live run over the same fixtures appended a duplicate set, doubled committed stake, and turned `roi` and `strike_rate` into fiction. That was survivable while `bankroll.json` did not exist. It has existed since this morning and now holds five settled bets, which makes a repeat run the easiest remaining way to destroy the only record of what was staked and what came back. With real fixtures about to load, the second half of the session is a status pass rather than a build: what is actually ready, and what only looks it.

### What makes two bets the same bet

The guard keys on **fixture + market + selection + match date**, and is deliberately blind to status. A settled bet is still a bet already on the book; re-pricing a match that has been graded is the worst duplicate of the lot, not an exempt one.

Two things had to be true first. Bets carried no match date — only `placed_at` and `settled_at`, both wall-clock times of the run — while `find_edges` reduced a fixture to `"Home v Away"` and threw away the `date` the feed supplies. So `Bet` and `StakeDecision` gain `match_date`, plumbed from the fixture feed through `size_bet`. It is defaulted to `''`, which is what lets the five records already on disk load unchanged: no migration, and `bankroll.json` is not rewritten. `''` means *unknown*, and the guard reads unknown as "cannot rule out" — such a record matches on the remaining three keys and is refused, rather than being assumed to belong to some other fixture.

`normalise_fixture()` moved from `settle_results.py` into `bankroll.py`. Bet identity is the ledger's business, and the duplicate guard and the settlement matcher must not be able to disagree about when two fixture names are one match.

The check runs under `--dry-run` too. Seeing the collision *before* the live run is the entire point of having a dry run; a guard that only spoke up while writing would report the problem exactly one step too late.

### --force appends, it never overwrites

`--force` is the re-price case: the odds moved and the operator wants the new price on the book. It takes a **pending** duplicate, appends a new bet, and marks the old one superseded — in both directions, `supersedes` and `superseded_by`.

Structural fields rather than a note, because `settle()` rewrites `notes` wholesale (`bankroll.py:158`). A prose-only link naming the superseded id would survive right up until the forced bet was graded, and then vanish — the link would break at the exact moment the ledger started depending on it.

Nothing is mutated or deleted. The superseded bet keeps its status, stake, odds and settlement; it gains a back-link and nothing else. The append-only guarantee holds, and a wrong `--force` is visible in a diff and revertible.

But a superseded bet leaves the record entirely. A re-price is one position, not two, and counting both would show two stakes and two results where the operator took one. Everything `summary()` reports now derives from `counted_bets` rather than `self.bets` — including `realised_pnl`, `bankroll` and `committed`, not only the win/loss columns. `bets_total` still names the size of the file and a new `bets_superseded` reconciles the difference, so the exclusion is stated rather than hidden.

`--force` does **not** override a settled duplicate, and no second flag is offered that would. Re-pricing a match that has already been graded is an error every time — it is precisely the state on disk right now, five settled bets against fixtures played in May — and a flag should not put it one keystroke away. It skips with its own reason line naming the bet and its status.

The strongest available test of all of this was the real ledger. `--dry-run` against `data/bankroll.json` catches all five standing bets through the unknown-date fallback *and* refuses them as settled, in one pass, and comes back with the file byte-identical.

The promoted-team naming risk is the one finding that could silently cost money. Seeding is real and correct, but it is only reachable under the exact stored key. `current_elo.json` holds `"Hull City"` and `"Coventry City"`, while the alias table in `odds_calculator.py` — whose own convention is short names, `Wolves`, `Leicester`, `Newcastle`, `Leeds`, and `Ipswich Town` → `Ipswich` — has no entry for either. Doing it now would mean carrying a half-live ledger for an unknown number of days; doing it at the moment of the first real run makes the boundary between exercise and track record a single, dated act. The 24 May data stays where it is until then.
- **Scrapers are fixed but unproven against 2026/27 pages.** Both repairs were confirmed against live 2025/26 pages and are covered by DOM-simulator tests that never touch the network, so nothing here catches the site drifting over the summer. `matches_data.json` ends 2026-05-19. The first scrape of the new season is the real test, and it should be run early enough that a break is not discovered on matchday.

## 2026-08-20 — Settlement: closing the loop on the ledger

**Branch:** `claude/odds-calculator-settlement-hvf6an` · **PR:** not yet opened

**Goal:** Make the ledger actually settle. `Ledger.settle()` had been written and unit-tested since Day 2 but nothing outside its own test ever called it, so every bet the pipeline struck stayed `pending` forever and `realised_pnl` / `strike_rate` / `roi` were structurally incapable of being anything but 0 and `null`. Until this session `data/bankroll.json` had never been written at all — not on disk, not anywhere in git history.

### The mechanism: manual results file, not a scraper

`python3 -m pipeline.settle_results` reads `data/results.json` — one entry per fixture, typed up by the operator after full time — grades each pending bet against it and calls `Ledger.settle()`.

Manual entry rather than a results scraper, deliberately. A settlement scraper is a second live-site dependency of exactly the kind that has already broken twice this week (the Stats tab, then the cookie banner), and it would sit on the path that moves money in the ledger rather than the one that gathers stats. Ten scorelines a week is a minute of typing against a component that would need the same DOM-drift maintenance the existing scrapers do. There is a bridge for later: `--from-matches` grades from the repo's own scraped store, `matches_data.json`, so once the existing scrapers refresh it the results are already in the building and no new scraper is needed.

A separate command from `run_pipeline`, not a flag on it. Pricing runs before kickoff against odds; settlement runs after full time against results. Fused, a re-priced run would also re-grade the book, and a settlement run would demand fixtures and odds it has no use for.

### It refuses to guess

The design rule is that silence is never a result. A bet with no entry in either source stays `pending` and is printed by id. A selection with no grading rule (`correct_score_2_1`) is reported, not graded — an ungraded bet must never fall through to `lost`, which is what "default to loss" quietly does to the PnL. An unreadable scoreline leaves the bet open. `--from-matches` will only settle a bet against a match played **on or after** the date the bet was struck, so a previous season's meeting of the same fixture cannot settle this season's bet. `--dry-run` grades the whole book and writes nothing; `--set BET_ID=STATUS` is the hand override for voids and corrections, and it is validated before anything is written.

### First real settlement

Priced the 24 May 2026 final matchday (the fixtures standing in `upcoming_fixtures.json`), which placed 5 bets at a total stake of 143.41, then settled all 5 against the real results:

| Bet | Fixture | Selection | Odds | Result | Outcome | PnL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 00001 | Crystal Palace v Arsenal | away | 1.77 | 1-2 | won | +14.75 |
| 00002 | Nott'm Forest v Bournemouth | home | 3.26 | 1-1 | lost | −24.43 |
| 00003 | Brighton v Man Utd | away | 3.59 | 0-3 | won | +77.23 |
| 00004 | Spurs v Everton | away | 3.87 | 1-0 | lost | −35.80 |
| 00005 | West Ham v Leeds | away | 3.79 | 3-0 | lost | −34.21 |

`realised_pnl` −2.46, `strike_rate` 0.40, `roi` −0.0172 on 143.41 staked. All three populate; none is `null`. The next pricing run sizes Kelly against 997.54 instead of 1000.00, which is the whole point — the loop from stake to result to next stake is closed for the first time.

`summary()` now also carries `roi_pct` (−1.72) beside the existing `roi` fraction. Same number, and anything display-facing wants the percentage.

`data/bankroll.json` is committed rather than gitignored. It is state, not a derived artifact: it is the only record of what was staked and what came back, and `pipeline_report.json` is gitignored precisely because it *is* regenerable from it. Versioning it means a wrong settlement is visible in a diff and revertible.

### Flagged, not fixed

- **The scorelines were sourced by web lookup, not typed by the operator.** Each was cross-checked against ESPN plus one independent source and the citation is stored per entry in `data/results.json`. They should be eyeballed once before the ledger is trusted as a track record; `--set` corrects any bet.
- **`upcoming_fixtures.json` is three months stale.** It still held 24 May fixtures on 20 August, so these bets were struck at odds three months after the matches were played. Fine as a settlement exercise, worthless as a track record. The odds refresh has to run before the ledger means anything.
- **`run_pipeline` has no idempotency guard.** Re-running it live against the same fixtures appends a second set of identical bets; nothing checks whether a fixture/selection is already on the book. Not settlement's problem to fix, but it is now the easiest way to corrupt a ledger that finally has real state in it.

## 2026-08-20 — Day 2 follow-up: cookie banner, API key handling

**Branch:** `claude/advanced-stats-cookie-banner-283t72` · **PR:** not yet opened

**Goal:** Close out the one Task 1 loose thread left from Day 2 — advanced stats were still blocked on a cookie-consent banner that would not dismiss — and remove the last hardcoded-secrets flag by moving the-odds-api.com keys out of source.

### The cookie banner

Live debugging thread, several rounds already in on this branch before this session: locator coverage was fixed, then locator priority order was fixed, and neither was it. The banner still never went away, and `accept_cookies()` printed no failure warning — meaning some locator matched and WebDriver's `.click()` did not raise, yet the page did not change.

**The success criterion was the bug.** `element.click()` not raising is not evidence anything happened. WebDriver raises on a click it could not *deliver* (intercepted, stale, not interactable); it does not raise on a click delivered to an element that ignored it. Three ordinary page conditions produce exactly that:

- a transparent duplicate of the banner earlier in document order — `is_displayed()` reports `opacity:0` as displayed, so a decoy is what `find_element()` hands back, clicks cleanly, and reports success;
- a server-rendered button whose consent handler hasn't hydrated yet — the click lands on the right element with no listener on it;
- a button with something painted on top of it.

Reproduced the first two against a DOM simulator (`tests/test_cookie_dismissal.py`) and confirmed they produce the reported signature exactly under the old code: returns `True`, banner still up, nothing printed. The third — occlusion — was ruled out: it would have printed the intercepted-click warning that was never seen live.

Fixed: `accept_cookies()` now verifies against the page after every click and only returns `True` once no clickable banner is left, escalating per candidate (native click → scripted click, which no overlay can block → retry after a pause for late hydration) before moving to the next candidate. A JS probe measures each candidate's box, computed style and the topmost element at its click point, so decoys are excluded before they're clicked and a covering element is named rather than guessed at. `cookie_banner_present()` now means "visible", not "in the markup" — the old existence check would have answered `True` just as loudly after a successful dismissal, since every CMP dismisses by hiding. A click that changes nothing is now a loud, diagnosable failure instead of a silent success that resurfaced later as an unexplained `ElementClickInterceptedException` on the Stats tab; that tab-click failure itself now names the actual blocking element instead of suggesting a suspect. Selenium is no longer imported by the dismissal path at all, so the whole flow is testable without a browser. Confirmed working against the live site under `ODDS_HEADLESS=0`.

### API keys out of source

Three the-odds-api.com keys were hardcoded in `odds_calculator.py` and public in this repo's git history — flagged Day 1, carried over at Day 2, fixed now. Keys now load from a gitignored `.env` (`.env.example` documents the two variables) or the real environment, via a small hand-rolled loader (`env_config.py`) — not `python-dotenv`, to avoid adding a dependency for something this small, same call made for `GeckoDriverManager` in `scrapers/browser.py`.

Only two keys are kept going forward, `ODDS_API_KEY` and `ODDS_API_KEY_BACKUP`, and — unlike before, where the list held three keys but only `API_KEYS[0]` was ever read — the backup is now wired into an actual failover: `fetch_bookmaker_odds()` switches to it automatically the moment the primary reports itself rate-limited or out of quota (HTTP 429, or 401 with `error_code: OUT_OF_USAGE_CREDITS`), and stays on it for the rest of the run rather than re-trying the dead key on every subsequent request. A 401 for any other reason (a genuinely bad key) is left alone rather than masked by a failover that can't fix it.

207 tests passing (183 before this session, 24 new: `tests/test_cookie_dismissal.py` + `tests/test_env_config.py`). Day 2 scope is now fully closed, including the flagged secrets item; the band-model coarseness flag from Day 2 remains open, deferred to Day 3+ as originally noted.

## 2026-08-15 — Day 1: Model & Maths Core

**Branch:** `claude/odds-calc-band1-audit-su1rn4` · **PR:** [#1](https://github.com/ferret-stack/odds-calculator/pull/1)

**Goal:** Close the ELO Band 1 gate before anything downstream (pipeline, distribution) is trusted. Locked scope: root-cause audit, fix + validate, promoted-team seeding.

### What was wrong

Went in with one hypothesis (a pre-`+284`-offset `1500` fallback landing on the current scale unadjusted). That hypothesis was correct but incomplete — the actual bug was four separate defects compounding into one symptom:

1. **`current_elo` was never loaded from disk.** `load_existing_data()` only loaded `matches_data.json`. Since the main pipeline scrapes before it updates ratings, every scraped match fell back to `1500` on *both* sides, regardless of whether the teams were actually known. 68 matches (21% of last season) ended up with both teams at 1500.
2. **The fallback was on the wrong scale.** The `+284` cosmetic offset isn't applied as a step in the code — it's baked into whatever's persisted in `current_elo.json` and carried forward by loading it as the baseline each run. A hardcoded literal never passes through that state, so `1500` sat 284 points below league average.
3. **Exact ELO ties were silently mislabelled.** All three places that determined match winners used `"stronger" if home_elo > away_elo else "weaker"` — no tie case. An exact tie means `elo_diff == 0`, i.e. always Band 1, so every tied match got miscounted as a weaker-team win. 50 phantom upsets, concentrated entirely in Band 1.
4. **The self-repair logic had become a permanent no-op.** `update_elo_ratings()` picked matches to reprocess by comparing match date against the last `elo_history` entry. Six matches carrying a corrupted `2026-12-30` date poisoned that comparison — every real match started comparing as "already done," so nothing ever ran again. It also stamped *post-match* (not pre-match) ratings onto records, which makes band membership a function of the result itself, and never recomputed the winner label after rewriting the ELOs — leaving 197 records contradicting their own stored ratings.

Also found along the way: **387 corrupt match dates** (17.5% of the dataset) — mostly day/month transposed (`04-10` read as April 10 instead of 4 October), confirmed against real fixtures (Aston Villa 7-2 Liverpool was really 4 Oct 2020, not 10 Apr). Needed fixing because the ELO chain is order-dependent — replaying it out of sequence corrupts every rating downstream of the error.

### What was fixed

- `current_elo` and `elo_history` now load at startup.
- Fallback ratings (for anyone still missing a rating) come from `league_baseline_elo()` — the live average of current ratings — never a bare literal.
- Added `classify_winner()` with an explicit `even` outcome, excluded from band stronger/draw/weaker rates rather than dumped into "weaker."
- Replaced the poisoned date-comparison filter with an explicit per-match `elo_processed` flag.
- Match records now stamp *pre-match* ratings (what was knowable before kickoff), not post-match.
- ELO chain now replays in `match_id` order rather than date order — IDs are assigned per matchweek in fixture sequence and are immune to the date corruption.
- Repaired the 387 corrupt dates (day/month swap detection + one rolled-over year), verified against real results.

New tooling:
- `tools/rebuild_elo.py` — full idempotent rebuild: repairs dates, replays the ELO chain from scratch, applies promoted-team seeding, regenerates all derived JSON.
- `tools/validate_elo.py` — rating-swing distribution, band W/D/L split, integrity checks (ties mislabelled, both-sides-1500, label/rating mismatches, bad dates). This is the standard validation harness going forward — run it after any change to the ELO pipeline.

### Validation — before / after

| Rating swings >50pts | 775 | **0** |
| Rating swings >100pts | 351 | **0** |
| Largest single swing | 556 | **30** |
| Mean \|swing\| | 33.99 | **6.77** |
| Band 1 — stronger team wins | 29.59% | **41.15%** |
| Band 1 — draw | 27.34% | 24.96% |
| Band 1 — weaker team wins | 43.07% | **33.89%** |
| Band 1 stronger-minus-weaker gap | −13.48pp | **+7.26pp** |

Swing ceiling is the sanity check that matters most: with K=20 and MOV capped near 1.7, no single match can move a team more than ~34 points. 775 swings above 50 were mathematically impossible — proof the old chain was corrupted, not just noisy.

### Promoted-team seeding

Hull City, Coventry City, Ipswich Town seeded at **1685** — average current (post-offset) ELO of 2025-26's bottom four: Spurs 1744, West Ham 1733, Wolves 1648, Burnley 1615.

Runs through its own `seed_team()` path, deliberately separate from the generic fallback fixed above — confirmed a deliberate seed can't be confused with a default silently firing.

Landed 18th–20th of the 2026-27 field. Fixtures spread across bands 1–7 — not artificially parked in Band 1, not inflated.

### Flagged, not touched

- **Band boundary off-by-one.** `elo_diff // 50 + 1` puts an exact diff of 50 in Band 2, but Band 1 is labelled "0-50" everywhere. Affects 2.2% of matches. Left alone deliberately — fixing it reassigns matches at every band edge, not just Band 1, and that's a judgement call, not an audit finding.
- **Dataset gaps in recent seasons**: 355 / 344 / 368 matches for 2023-24 / 2024-25 / 2025-26 vs. 380 expected — some matches missing from the scrape.
- **Three API keys hardcoded** in `odds_calculator.py` and now public in git history. Worth rotating regardless of this PR.

### Status

Day 1 gate closed. Day 2 (Automated Pipeline) is unblocked.
